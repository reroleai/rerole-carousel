One question in the last 5 minutes can undo a 45-minute interview: "So, what does your company do?"

It happens right after they ask if you have any questions for them. Most people treat that bit as a formality. Wrong. Interviewers write down what you ask, and your interview questions get read as closely as your answers.

The ones that hurt are ordinary. Asking what the company does when their LinkedIn page and last 3 press releases are one search away. Opening with salary, 24 days of leave or 2 WFH days. "Did I get it?" while 4 other candidates are still booked that week. Asking about promotion before day 1.

Pay and remote work are fair to ask about. Just later, with the recruiter or at the offer stage.

Ask about the work instead. What does success look like at 90 days? What's the hardest part of this role? My favourite of all the questions to ask the interviewer is the closer: is there anything in my background that makes you hesitate? You get to fix the doubt while you're still in the room, not in an email 2 days later.

Save this for the night before your next interview.
